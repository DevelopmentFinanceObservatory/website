## How to read this

Pick a donor, a year and a price basis above the chart, and every panel follows them. Group totals, such as DAC countries, come after the countries in the donor list.

Each bar is one count of the same donor's ODA in the same year, on one scale. The text under a bar says what the count counts, put together from the terms in the DFO's graph.

- Gross disbursements are what the donor paid out, with nothing taken off.
- Net disbursements take off loan repayments and other amounts the donor received.
- Grant equivalents count a grant in full and a loan only for its concessional part. They count private sector instruments and debt relief on a different basis from gross disbursements.
- Commitments are firm promises to pay. They can run ahead of or behind disbursements.
- The OECD headline is the figure the OECD reports as a donor's ODA.

The two bridges show where the gaps between gross disbursements and the other two disbursement counts come from.

The chart over time draws each count from 2015, with the chosen year shaded. The OECD headline is the pale band under the line it follows: net disbursements to 2017, grant equivalents from 2018.

The last panel splits gross disbursements into bilateral aid and core contributions to multilateral agencies, gives in-donor refugee costs as a share of the headline, and sets the headline against the UN target of 0.7% of gross national income.

To compare donors, or a donor against the target of 0.7% of gross national income, use the OECD headline. To see how much a donor paid out in a year, use gross disbursements, or net disbursements to take off what came back. Do not compare the headline across 2018, when its basis changed.

## Notes

- Amounts received, the step from gross to net disbursements, are computed as gross minus net disbursements. The DFO publishes no separate series for them.
- In 2018 the OECD switched its headline from net disbursements to grant equivalents. The headline bar shows net disbursements to 2017 and grant equivalents from 2018, so its figures either side of the switch are not comparable. The grant-equivalent bridge starts in 2018 for the same reason.
- The gap between gross disbursements and grant equivalents is mostly loans, but it can be larger than a donor's loans, or negative. The United Kingdom's grant equivalents are above its gross disbursements from 2023.
- Net disbursements can sit above gross, as for Finland in 2020. The bridge then shows the step as an addition.
- The sentence under the grant-equivalent bridge uses gross non-grant disbursements, which run from 2018 to 2024. It appears only when the gap is no larger than those disbursements.
- Constant prices are in 2024 US dollars. The DFO converts each count with the OECD DAC deflators, from ONE's conversion tables. The deflators take out each donor's inflation and its exchange-rate movements against the dollar. In 2024 the two price bases give the same figures. A donor with no deflator for a year shows "no constant-price figure".
- 2025 figures are the OECD's preliminary data. Most donors have no 2025 commitments or splits yet. The OECD adds them with the final figures.
- A group total, such as DAC countries, shows no figure for a year in which fewer than half of the countries that usually report a series reported it, because the total would sum only part of the group. For the same reason, group totals have no grant equivalents before 2018. In 2025 group totals have no commitments, splits or refugee costs, because only two countries reported them.
