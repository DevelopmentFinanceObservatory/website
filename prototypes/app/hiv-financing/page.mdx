HIV aid is gross ODA disbursements under CRS purpose code 13040, STD control including HIV/AIDS, in current US dollars. The code covers all sexually transmitted diseases, and it holds about $467m of ODA loans to countries from 2015 to 2024, mostly from World Bank IDA ($303m) and the Asian Development Bank ($164m). HIV work recorded under other codes, such as health systems or reproductive health, is left out.

The Global Fund reports to the CRS as a provider, so money donors give the Global Fund is counted once, under the Global Fund. It also records part of its HIV money under other codes: combined HIV and tuberculosis grants under code 12250 from 2015 to 2019, and multi-disease grants under code 12110 from 2020. Only part of each of these grants is for HIV, so the chart draws them hatched above the bars and leaves them out of the total, and the table marks the countries that received them in {LAST_YEAR}. From 2019 to 2020 its multi-disease money to countries fell from {gfMultiToCountries(2019)} to {gfMultiToCountries(2020)}, while its money under the HIV code rose from {gfToCountries(2019)} to {gfToCountries(2020)}, so much of the jump in its bars between those years is a change of code. A dashed line marks it.

Figures are in current US dollars, with no adjustment for inflation, and follow the timing of donors' reports. US money to countries fell from {usToCountries(2018)} in 2018 to {usToCountries(2019)} in 2019 and rose to {usToCountries(2020)} in 2020, so read single-year changes with care.

The data cover official donors. Private foundations, such as the Gates Foundation, are not counted. KFF counts donor governments' HIV spending, including their contributions to the Global Fund, so its totals differ from these.
